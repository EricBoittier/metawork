## Throughput vs dataset size, and DDP vs FSDP (2026-10-15)

Struct/s falls from 5379 (2^20) to 4251 (2^24). The cause is **the work per structure**, not the parallelism. Larger MAD-CORE subsets contain bigger and denser structures. - Sync is constant at ~4 ms with Muon and ~1 ms with Adam (145179). Moving the 12 MB gradient and weight blobs costs < 1 ms per step. The other ~3 ms of the Muon sync is Newton-Schulz compute on GPU 0.

**DDP vs FSDP: keep data parallel with a replicated model (DDP).** FSDP solves a memory problem, and we don't have one. The model is 3.04 M parameters (12 MB fp32, 36 MB with optimizer state), and peak use is 4.7-7.1 GB of a 96 GB card. FSDP would add an all-gather in forward, a second one in backward and a reduce-scatter on every step. That is about 3x DDP's communication, over PCIe, in exchange for saving a few tens of MB. The mildest form of sharding has already been measured: ZeRO-1 (sharded optimizer, 145177/145178). It was slower: Adam 5091 vs 5801 struct/s, Muon x10 4894 vs 5008, because of uneven shards (64/63 Adam, 78/49 Muon) plus the all-gather. Strictly, the current scheme is not textbook DDP either. Gradients are reduced onto GPU 0, the optimizer runs there, and the weight blob is broadcast back ("parameter server on GPU 0"). On 2 GPUs this is as good as an all-reduce, because Newton-Schulz compute dominates the sync, not communication.

**Where the remaining time goes, and what could help:**
1. Compute (~70 % of the step) is the model kernels themselves. This is the main lever and has nothing to do with the parallel scheme.
2. **Optimizer preload: tested, and the straggler was a side issue.** `make_step_batches` gave worker 0 a head start of the sync time (~4 ms with Muon), although the optimizer runs only after every worker has finished. Removing it (145263) shrank the Muon gap by 0.5 ms but made the packing slower overall (Adam -4 %, Muon -1 %). The preload had also loosened the per-micro-batch cost cap (1.05 x (total + P)/W), which meant fewer micro-batches at ~4 ms fixed cost each. Replacement: try several caps (uncapped, 1.0-1.75 x total/W) and keep the plan whose longest-first deal finishes first. Under test in 145265.
3. The fixed cost per micro-batch (~4.1 ms in the fitted model) times 1.41 micro-batches per worker; the packing already trades this off against padding.
4. When scaling to more GPUs, the serial sync on GPU 0 grows with W (staging copies plus broadcast). At that point, use a real all-reduce with a replicated optimizer, or shard only the Newton-Schulz work evenly by FLOPs (ZeRO-1 split by tensor count was unbalanced). Not worth doing on 2 GPUs.
